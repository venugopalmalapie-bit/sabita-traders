Not eligible for the tractor subsidy? Here is what you can still do

Most reasons for missing the Odisha tractor subsidy are temporary or can be fixed. If the target was full, book in the next release. If your permit lapsed, apply again after 3 months. If you took a subsidy in the last 5 years, you can get it again once the 5 years are over, at a reduced amount. If you can’t wait, a tractor loan with a trade-in or a co-applicant can get you a tractor this season.

By M Krishna Reddy, Managing Director, Sabita Traders · Published 20 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

  • Permit lapsed? Apply again 3 months after it expired.

  • No land in your name? Not a barrier for the subsidy: you need a Farmer ID, Aadhaar and mobile number.
  • Can’t wait? Buy now with a tractor loan, a trade-in or a co-applicant.

1. “I don’t have a Farmer ID”

You don’t need to own land. Odisha’s farm mechanisation rules say every farmer registered with Krushak Odisha and Go-Sugam, with a valid Farmer ID, Aadhaar and mobile number, can apply whatever the size of their land.

  • Register on krushak.odisha.gov.in, then on sugam.odisha.gov.in.
  • If you farm leased or shared land, ask your Krushi Sahayak or Block Agriculture Officer which papers to show.
  • Link your Aadhaar to your Farmer ID and to your bank account. The subsidy is paid into that account.

2. “The target was full”

Subsidy slots are released in rounds, and Ganjam’s share can fill within hours. The Agriculture Department’s notice of 31 Oct 2026 lists the next tractor target releases as 1 Dec and 15 Dec 2026, and 1 Jan and 15 Jan 2027, with booking reopening at 3 PM.

  • Keep your documents and loan file ready before release day.
  • Decide your model in advance: ₹1,20,000 subsidy up to 48 HP, ₹1,50,000 from 50 HP (see which models).
  • WhatsApp us before the release date and we help you book at 3 PM.
  • If the crop season can’t wait, buy now with a loan (section 6). Weigh the subsidy against the cost of a lost season.

3. “My permit lapsed”

A permit is valid for 30 days if you pay yourself and 45 days with a bank loan. Under clause 5.11 of the official rules, a farmer whose booking or permit lapses must apply again after three months from the date the last permit expired.

  • Use the 3 months to get the bank loan approved in principle, so the bank can issue its delivery order quickly next time.
  • Note the date you can apply again, and book on the first target release after it.

4. “I took a subsidy in the last 5 years”

This is not a lifetime ban. Clause 2.1 of the 2026 rules allows the subsidy again on the same machine after its 5-year moratorium, at 100%, 75% and 50% of the normal assistance for the first, second and third purchase.

Remember: a subsidised tractor must stay with you for 5 years. Selling it earlier can lead to recovery of the subsidy.

5. “My implement subsidy was refused”

Tractor-drawn implements are subsidised only for farmers who own a tractor, and each implement type can be claimed once per tractor registration (RC).

  • Buy the tractor first, then apply for the implement on your own RC. See our implements.
  • If you need an implement only a few weeks a year, renting may cost less (section 8).

6. “I can’t wait. How do I buy without the subsidy?”

A tractor loan is the most common route. The lender pays most of the price and you pay the rest as a down payment.

What to keep ready:

  • CIBIL score above 700. This is what our finance partners generally look for.
  • Aadhaar, PAN, photographs, bank statements and income proof. Land records help where you have them.
  • Down payment money. A bigger down payment means a smaller EMI and often an easier approval.

Ways to pay less upfront:

  • Trade in your old tractor. Its value goes towards the down payment. Check your exchange value.
  • Keep your Kisan Credit Card for crop costs. KCC crop loans are at 7%, and paying on time earns 3% more interest relief, on limits up to ₹3 lakh. Using KCC for seed and fertiliser keeps your own cash free for the down payment. KCC itself is not a tractor loan.
  • Compare lenders. We work with 11 finance partners, including John Deere Financial, SBI, HDFC Bank and Shriram Finance, so you get more than one offer.

7. “My loan was rejected”

  • Check your CIBIL report. You can get one free report a year from CIBIL. Look for old unpaid dues or mistakes and get them corrected.
  • Clear overdue EMIs and give your score time to improve.
  • Add a co-applicant, such as a family member with a good credit record and income.
  • Pay a bigger down payment so the lender takes less risk.
  • Know the ₹2 lakh rule. RBI asks banks to waive collateral and margin on agricultural loans up to ₹2 lakh per borrower. Tractor loans are usually bigger, so expect the lender to ask for security on the amount above that.

8. Other routes: buy as a group, or rent

Buy through a group. Pani Panchayats, women’s self-help groups and cooperatives can apply for the subsidy through a nominated member. Under clause 4.1, up to 33% of targets can go to women’s self-help groups, at 75% of the normal subsidy for selected commercially viable machines. The subsidy is lower, but it is a separate share of the target when the general one is full.

Group credit for landless and small farmers. Odisha’s Balaram scheme helps landless, small and marginal farmers form groups of at least 5 farmers to borrow from banks together. These amounts suit crop inputs and small equipment better than a whole tractor.

Rent. Pani Panchayats, self-help groups and cooperatives near you may run hiring centres that rent tractors and implements by the hour or by the acre. On a small farm, renting can cost less than an EMI.

Want to run a tractor hire business? The central Agriculture Infrastructure Fund supports Custom Hiring Centres with at least 4 machines or implements. That is a business plan rather than a personal purchase, and we will cover it in a separate guide.

Mistakes that cost farmers the subsidy next time

  1. Booking before the bank loan is ready, so the 45-day permit lapses.
  2. Aadhaar not linked to the Farmer ID or the bank account.
  3. Choosing the model only on release day, after the slots are gone.
  4. Selling a subsidised tractor before 5 years.
  5. Applying for an implement before the tractor is registered in your name.

Questions farmers ask us

Can I get a tractor subsidy a second time in Odisha?

Yes, after the 5-year waiting period for that machine. The official rules pay 100% of the normal subsidy the first time, 75% the second time and 50% the third time.

Can a tenant farmer or a farmer without land get the tractor subsidy?

Odisha’s rules let any farmer registered on Krushak Odisha and Go-Sugam, with a valid Farmer ID, Aadhaar and mobile number, apply regardless of land size. If you farm leased land, ask your Block Agriculture Officer which papers to show for the Farmer ID.

My subsidy permit lapsed. When can I apply again?

Three months after the date your last permit expired.

When are the next tractor subsidy target releases in Ganjam?

Per the Agriculture Department notice of 31 Oct 2026: 1 Dec and 15 Dec 2026, and 1 Jan and 15 Jan 2027. Tractor booking reopens at 3 PM on those days.

Can I buy a tractor on loan without the subsidy?

Yes. Most lenders look for a CIBIL score above 700, KYC and income proof, and a down payment. A trade-in or a co-applicant can make approval easier.

Is a tractor loan collateral-free?

RBI asks banks to waive collateral and margin on agricultural loans up to ₹2 lakh per borrower. Tractor loans are usually bigger, so lenders normally ask for security on the amount above that.

Can I use my Kisan Credit Card to buy a tractor?

No. KCC is short-term credit for crop and allied costs. Using it for those costs can keep your own cash free for the tractor down payment.
